# Iceberg Loop Engineering Review

## Review 1

Issue: A standalone app depends on user self-control. Users may skip it when most impulsive.

Adjustment: Start with PWA for speed, but treat browser extension, mobile shortcut, and app blocker as core roadmap items rather than optional extras.

Issue: Too many questions can cause drop-off.

Adjustment: Keep the first check under 60 seconds. Ask only the essential questions first. Move portfolio details, advanced rules, and journaling notes into optional follow-up screens.

Issue: Language can sound like financial advice.

Adjustment: Never say "buy" or "do not buy" as an investment recommendation. Say "this violates your rules," "this appears behaviorally risky," or "cooldown recommended."

Issue: AI could hallucinate market facts.

Adjustment: AI should analyze the user's written reason and behavior history only. Market data and rule scoring should come from explicit inputs or reliable APIs.

## Review 2

Issue: Trading journals can copy this feature.

Adjustment: Differentiate with pre-trade friction, cooldowns, delayed rule changes, and app-entry interception. The product is not a journal; the journal is evidence for the friction system.

Issue: Beginners may not know they need discipline.

Adjustment: Market the product around avoiding regret, not discipline education. The hook is "stop the trade you will regret," not "become a rational investor."

Issue: Risk score may feel arbitrary.

Adjustment: Always show triggered reasons and the user's own rule violations. The explanation matters more than the number.

Issue: The target market is broad.

Adjustment: Start with people who already identify as impulsive traders. They have stronger pain and willingness to pay. Expand to beginners after messaging is proven.

Issue: v1 without brokerage integration may feel weak.

Adjustment: Make the ritual strong: approval flow, cooldown timer, journal proof, browser extension roadmap, and personal evidence feedback.
